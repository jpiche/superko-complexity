# C-58 — a board of the mover's stones alone follows a pass

**This file is the proof, and it is not machine-checked.** Its one step about
plays is `Basic.resolve_self`, kernel-checked; the argument over a game is
written out here and nowhere else, as `proofs/C-52.md`'s is. The ledger
records C-58 as `proved` with formalization `formalizable`: proved by hand,
checked by no kernel.

## The claim

Play a game from `Superko.start b₀ c₀` under either superko rule, and let
`Sₜ = ⟨Bₜ, Cₜ⟩` be a situation of it, `t ≥ 1`.

1. If `Bₜ` carries a stone and every stone it carries is `Cₜ`'s, the move
   `mvₜ₋₁` was a pass.
2. On 1×6 from the empty board, every game reaching `X.X.X.` with Black to
   move and not ended reaches it after a White pass with the pass count one,
   and Black then wins at every komi floor below 6, under either rule, by
   passing.

## Proof

**1.** `Situation.after` hands the turn to the other color on a pass and on a
play alike, so `Cₜ₋₁ = Cₜ.other`. Suppose `mvₜ₋₁` were a play at `p`. Then
`Bₜ = resolve Bₜ₋₁ Cₜ₋₁ p`, and `Basic.resolve_self` says `Bₜ p = some Cₜ₋₁`:
the board carries a stone of `Cₜ.other`. It does not, so `mvₜ₋₁` is a pass.
(The step uses nothing about suicide: `resolve` never removes the mover's own
stone, and `Suicide.Forbid` refuses a move rather than clearing it.)

**2.** `X.X.X.` carries stones and all are Black's, so by 1 the last move was
a White pass. The game is not ended, so the pass count after it is exactly
one: `step` writes `passes + 1` on a pass, and `Ended` is `2 ≤ passes`. A pass
is always legal (`docs/formal-model.md` OPEN-1, C-18). Black's pass makes the
count two, ending the game at `X.X.X.`, whose area is 6 for Black and 0 for
White: each empty point of `X.X.X.` has only Black neighbors. At komi floor
`k < 6` Black wins there (`winnerZ_eq_winner`). So `WinsFor … black` holds at
`Sₜ` by the `mover` constructor with the pass.

## What is not proved

- **No kernel has checked 1 or 2.** Both range over a game as a sequence of
  moves, which the development does not define; C-52 has the same gap.
- **Nothing about 2×3.** `OOO/.X.` with White to move carries a Black stone,
  so 1 says nothing about how it is reached; a Black play at `(1,1)` reaches it
  from `OOO/...`. Whether some game reaching it makes the rules' values differ
  is experiment 006.
- **Nothing about the value under encoding (C).** C-56 stands: with the root
  situation alone archived and no pass pending, `X.X.X.` with Black to move is
  worth 1 under PSK and 6 under SSK. The two readings are different problems,
  `docs/formal-model.md` §5.
