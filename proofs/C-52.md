# C-52 — what separates positional from situational superko

**This file is the proof, and it is not machine-checked.** Its local steps —
C-50, C-51 and the two per-play facts about `resolve` that step 3 counts with —
are kernel-checked in
[`../lean/SuperkoComplexity/Results/C50_Mechanism.lean`](../lean/SuperkoComplexity/Results/C50_Mechanism.lean).
The argument that assembles them over a walk of moves is written out here
and nowhere else. The ledger records C-52 as `proved` with formalization
`formalizable`, and that pairing means exactly this: proved by hand, checked by
no kernel. It is the first row in that position.

## The claim

Play a game from `Superko.start b₀ c₀` under `Superko.SSK`: every move `mvᵢ`
with `i < t` is permitted by `SSK` at a state that has not ended. Write
`Sᵢ = ⟨Bᵢ, Cᵢ⟩` for the situation after `i` moves and `mvᵢ` for the move made
from it. Suppose the
play `mvₜ` is permitted by `Superko.SSK` and refused by `Superko.PSK`, and
write `b′ = Bₜ₊₁` for the board it creates. Let `α = Cₜ` be the mover and
`β = α.other`.

1. `b′` has stood earlier in the game, every time with `α` to play and never
   with `β` to play.
2. Let `u` be the last time `u ≤ t` with `Bᵤ = b′`. The walk
   `mvᵤ, …, mvₜ` has odd length `L = t + 1 − u ≥ 3`, its first and last moves
   are plays by `α`, and no pass is made at any time `v < t` at which the board
   is `b′`.
3. Let `a` and `b` be the numbers of plays by `α` and by `β` in the walk, `Kβ`
   the number of `α` stones removed during `β`'s plays, and `Kα` the number of
   `β` stones removed during `α`'s plays. Then `Kβ = a` and `Kα = b`, and hence
   `a ≥ 2`, `b ≥ 1` and the walk holds at least three plays.
4. If the walk holds exactly three plays and no pass, `β`'s single play removes
   exactly two `α` stones and `α`'s two plays remove one `β` stone between
   them.
5. Such plays occur in games with no pass at all. The separation of the two
   rules therefore does not depend on the pass exemption of
   `docs/formal-model.md` OPEN-1 and C-18.

Two things the claim does not say, because both are false. A board can come
back after two plays — one by each color, which is a ko — but then it comes
back with the same color to play, which both rules refuse. And a pass can sit
inside the walk: it is excluded only at the board `b′` itself, and only before
the separating play — after it, a pass at `b′` is as legal as any other.

## Proof

**The history.** `start` seeds `seen` with `S₀`, and `step` inserts the
situation it moves to, so after `t` moves `seen = {S₀, …, Sₜ}`.
`Situation.after` hands the turn to the other color on a pass and on a play
alike, so `Cᵢ = c₀` for even `i` and `c₀.other` for odd `i`.

**1.** `SSK` permits `mvₜ`, so `mvₜ` is a play whose board conditions
(`PlayableAt`) hold; `PSK` refuses it, so its repetition conjunct fails, and
some `Sᵢ` with `i ≤ t` has `Bᵢ = b′`. `SSK` permits it, so
`⟨b′, Cₜ₊₁⟩ = ⟨b′, β⟩` is not among `S₀, …, Sₜ`: every `i ≤ t` with
`Bᵢ = b′` has `Cᵢ = α`.

**2.** `u` exists by 1, and `Cᵤ = α = Cₜ`, so `u` and `t` have the same parity
and `L` is odd. `u ≠ t`: otherwise `mvₜ`, a play at an empty point of `Bₜ`,
would recreate the board it was played on, and `C51_play_changes_board` says
no play does. So `u ≤ t − 2` and `L ≥ 3`.

Suppose a pass were made at a time `v < t` with `Bᵥ = b′` (at `v = t` the move
is the play `mvₜ`). By `pass_seen_both`, the history after it, at time
`v + 1 ≤ t`, holds `⟨b′, black⟩` and
`⟨b′, white⟩`; histories only grow (`Basic.seen_subset_of_step`); so
`⟨b′, β⟩` would be in `seen` at time `t`, and `C50_pass_closes_board` says
`SSK` would refuse `mvₜ`. Hence no such pass. In particular `mvᵤ`, made at
board `b′`, is a play, and it is `α`'s since `Cᵤ = α`; `mvₜ` is a play by
`α` by hypothesis.

**3.** For a position `B` and color `x`, let `#x(B)` be the number of points
carrying `x`. Let `x` play at `p` on `B` with `B p = none` — which every play of
the walk satisfies, since each is permitted by `SSK` and so meets
`PlayableAt`'s first conjunct.

- The result's `x` stones are exactly `B`'s together with `p`. Away from `p`
  the result carries an `x` stone exactly where `B` does
  (`resolve_mover_of_ne`); at `p` it carries one (`Basic.resolve_self`), where
  `B` carried none. So `#x` rises by exactly one.
- The result's `x.other` stones are among `B`'s (`resolve_other_of_other`). So
  `#x.other` falls by the number of stones removed.
- A pass changes no point.

Sum over the walk. `Bᵤ = Bₜ₊₁ = b′`, so the net change in `#α` is zero, and it
is `a − Kβ`; likewise the net change in `#β` is `b − Kα`. So `Kβ = a` and
`Kα = b`. By 2, the walk's first and last moves are distinct plays by `α`, so
`a ≥ 2`. Then `Kβ ≥ 2 > 0`, and `α` stones are removed only during `β`'s
plays, so `b ≥ 1`. So `a + b ≥ 3`.

**4.** `a + b = 3` with `a ≥ 2` and `b ≥ 1` gives `a = 2` and `b = 1`, so
`Kβ = 2` and `Kα = 1`. With no pass, `L = 3`: `α` plays first and third, `β`
second.

**5.** A game under `Defs.lean`'s rules on the empty 1×3 board, Black to play,
with points numbered 0, 1, 2 from the left:

| move | board after | to play | removed |
|---|---|---|---|
| — | `...` | Black | — |
| Black 0 | `X..` | White | none |
| White 2 | `X.O` | Black | none |
| Black 1 | `XX.` | White | the White stone: its only neighbor now carries Black |
| White 2 | `..O` | Black | both Black stones: their one empty neighbor is now White |
| Black 0 | `X.O` | White | none |

Every play is at an empty point and leaves its own chain a liberty, so each is
`PlayableAt`. The first four boards are distinct, so the first four plays
recreate nothing. The fifth creates `⟨X.O, White⟩`, which has not occurred:
`X.O` stood once, after the second move, with Black to play. So `SSK` permits
the fifth move and `PSK` refuses it. Its walk runs from the second move's
board, with profile one, two and none removed, as 4 requires. The same line is
replayed by the Rust mirror in
`crates/superko-graph/tests/containment.rs`, test
`the_rules_differ_on_one_by_three_without_any_pass` (`computed`).

Nothing in 1 to 4 uses the pass exemption. The one step about passes, in 2,
uses what `step` archives on a pass, which is the same under either reading of
OPEN-1. The line in 5 holds no pass, so it is a game under either reading too.

**A pass inside a walk.** On the empty 2×2 board, Black to play, with rows
written top first: Black (0,0) `X./..`; White (0,1) `XO/..`; Black (1,0)
`XO/X.`; White passes; Black (1,1) `X./XX`, removing the White stone; White
(0,1) `.O/..`, removing three Black stones; Black (0,0) `XO/..`, with White to
play. `XO/..` stood after the second move with Black to play, so the last move
separates the rules. Its walk holds four plays and one pass, at a board other
than `XO/..`, with `a = 3`, `b = 1` and `Kβ = 3`. Traced by hand, and not by a
test.

## What is not proved

- **No kernel has checked 1 to 4.** The argument ranges over sequences of
  moves, and the development has no notion of a game as a sequence of states.
  Writing one is what formalizing this claim costs; the local steps it needs
  are already kernel-checked.
- **Nothing about values.** A legality gap is necessary for the value of a
  position to differ between the rules and is not sufficient. Under
  `Defs.lean`'s rules no position's winner differs on any board of at most five
  points at the komi floors tested (C-53, `computed`); positions whose winner
  differs exist on 1×6 and 2×3 (C-56, `computed`); which position is the least
  such is C-17, `open`.
- **The name.** The three-play case is the shape this project has called
  "sending two, returning one". No source for the name is held.

The statement above was reached by correcting an earlier draft whose "a board
never recurs after two plays" and "a pass cannot supply the parity" were both
refuted by counterexample; `notebook/2026-09-12-c17-separation-search.md`
keeps them.
