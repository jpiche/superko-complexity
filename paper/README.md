# Paper

The output. Nothing here yet, and nothing should be until something is proved.

## Shape

Short, and short *because* of the formalization rather than in spite of it. The
prose does not carry the burden of convincing, so it does not need the length
that carrying it would require.

The intended order is unusual and deliberate:

1. **The definitions**, in full. Go's rules are small enough to fit, and this is
   what a reader must actually check.
2. **The statement**, in one displayed box.
3. **The verification**: repository, commit, theorem name, and the `#print
   axioms` output. A reader who wants to check rather than read stops here.
4. **The proof sketch**, for the reader who wants the idea.
5. **What is not formalized**, explicitly, with citations for every step that
   rests on the literature.

Section 5 is not a weakness to be minimized. A clear statement of the trusted
boundary is what a careful reader wants and what a careless one omits, and for
an author with no institutional standing it is the most credible thing in the
document.

## Venues

Recorded so the choice is not made at the last minute: Computers and Games,
Advances in Computer Games, the Games of No Chance series, FUN with Algorithms,
MFCS; the CGT workshops at BIRS and Games at Dal; and for the formalization
itself, ITP or CPP.

Practical route for an unaffiliated author: arXiv preprint plus the repository,
then direct contact with the small number of people who have published on this.
The "People" section of
[`../references/README.md`](../references/README.md) records who they are, and
on what terms.
